Access keys and sign-in for apps

An access key is a long secret your code sends with every request, as Authorization: Bearer <key>. You make it under Settings → Other apps, choose what it may touch and when it runs out, and revoke it whenever you like. An app that connects on your behalf uses OAuth instead, so you never hand it a key at all.

How do you make a key?​

  1. Open Settings → Other apps and start a new access key.
  2. Name it so you will know it in three months. Applications spreadsheet says more than key 1.
  3. Choose its scopes and how long it lasts.
  4. The key shows once, with a copy button. Put it somewhere safe before you close the panel.

The list then shows each key's name, scopes, first few characters, when it was made and when it was last used, never the whole key. ClearHire emails you each time a key is made, so one you did not make does not slip past.

Can you see a key again?​

Yes, within 10 minutes of a fresh sign-in. The same rule covers a webhook signing secret and a calendar feed address.

WhenWhat happens
You signed in under 10 minutes agoThe key shows
You signed in longer agoClearHire says so and offers to sign you in again, then brings you back to the same row
Two-step sign-in is onYou also enter a code from your authenticator app, even inside the 10 minutes

Staying signed in for weeks is convenient, and it is also exactly what a laptop left open in a shared office looks like. A fresh sign-in is the proof that the person asking is you. A key is never sent by email.

What do the scopes mean?​

Each part of your account has up to three scopes:

ScopeLets the key
<part>:readRead it: your applications, interviews, reminders, contacts, resumes, job alerts, CV or profile
<part>:writeAdd to it and change it
<part>:deleteRemove from it

If you belong to a company on ClearHire, two more are offered, and both only read:

ScopeLets the key
company_jobs:readRead the jobs your company has posted
company_applications:readRead the applications sent to those jobs

There is no scope that posts a job, moves an applicant or edits a company. The exact name of every scope is in the OpenAPI file.

Write includes read. Remove is never included. A write scope lets the key read what it changes; nothing lets it remove unless you ticked that scope, which sits apart on the screen and starts unticked. A key's scopes never grow after it is made: when you need more, make a new key and revoke the old one.

How long does a key last?​

You choose 30, 90 or 365 days. After that date every request made with it answers 401.

How do you replace or revoke a key?​

  • Replace makes a new key. The old one keeps working for 24 hours while you swap the new one into your code, and is then revoked. Both sit in the list until then.
  • Revoke stops a key at once. The next request made with it answers 401.

Deleting your ClearHire account revokes every key, connected app, webhook and feed with it.

How does an app connect without a key?​

Through OAuth 2.1, the same sign-in an AI assistant uses. The app sends you to ClearHire's consent page, which shows the app's name and where it will send you back, the ClearHire account being connected, and what the app is asking for, in plain sentences grouped into read, write and remove, remove unticked to start. Approve and Deny sit side by side. With two-step sign-in on, approving asks for a code.

The app then appears under Settings → Other apps, where you can revoke it. Revoking ends its access on its next request, not whenever its sign-in would have run out. More on the consent page: AI assistants.

What are the rate limits?​

Each key and each connected app may send 120 requests a minute. On top of that your account has a daily total, set by your plan and shared by every key and app you have, so making more keys never raises it. Settings → Other apps shows both limits beside today's count; the plans themselves are on the pricing page.

HeaderMeans
RateLimit-LimitRequests allowed in the current window
RateLimit-RemainingRequests left in it
RateLimit-ResetSeconds until the window starts again

Past a limit the API answers 429 with a Retry-After header. Wait that many seconds, then try again.

What does an error look like?​

Every error, from every route, has the same shape:

{
  "error": {
    "code": "reauth_required",
    "message": "Sign in again to see this key.",
    "details": { "max_age_seconds": 600 },
    "correlation_id": "…"
  }
}

Program against code; message is for people and may be reworded. If you write to us about a failed request, quote the correlation_id. It is how we find that one request and nothing else.

StatusUsually means
401No key, a wrong one, or one that is revoked or expired
403The key lacks a scope the request needs (details names it), or a fresh sign-in is needed
404Nothing with that id on your account, including anything that belongs to someone else
429A rate limit was reached; see Retry-After

Every code is listed in the OpenAPI file.
