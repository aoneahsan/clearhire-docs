# Challenges, badges and levels

ClearHire has a progression layer: **challenges** you can finish, **badges** that record that you
did, **levels** that rise with your points, and a **leaderboard you have to opt into**. It exists to
make the slow, invisible parts of a job search visible — a profile kept current, a record confirmed
by a company, a question answered in the forums.

## The one rule worth reading first

> **Points come from finishing a challenge, never from opening the app, and they are never removed.
> There is no streak to break and nothing decays.**

That is not a summary. It is the design's own sentence, and it rules out the two mechanics people
most expect to find here. **ClearHire has no streaks.** Not a lapsed feature, not a planned one —
they are a stated non-goal, because a counter that punishes you for a quiet week is measuring your
attendance rather than your progress. Nothing you have earned can be taken away by not showing up.

## The mechanics

| Mechanic | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Challenges | Named pieces of real work, each with a rule for what counts |
| Badges | A permanent record that you finished one; every badge states its own requirement |
| Levels | Rise with total points, and unlock a little more of the product at each rung |
| Leaderboard | **Off unless you turn it on**, and it publishes only what the page says it will |

### Challenges

A challenge is a piece of work the product can actually check: fill in every part of your profile,
have a role countersigned by the company that employed you, get a forum reply marked as the answer.
Each one states the rule it counts by, so there is nothing to guess at.

### Badges

A badge records that a challenge was finished, with the date it happened. Some badges name the
company that countersigned the record behind them. **Every badge states its requirement whether or
not you hold it** — a badge that will not say what it takes is a lottery ticket.

Badges are not visible to employers. They are a record of what you did on ClearHire, not a
credential.

### Levels

Levels rise with points, and each rung unlocks something concrete rather than decorative — more
resume slots, more verification requests in flight at once. Your level is private unless you turn on
the one profile line that shows it.

### The leaderboard

**It is off by default and you have to opt in.** When you do, the board publishes your display name
as it appears on your public profile, your level, your points and how many roles you have confirmed —
the count, not which companies. It never publishes anything you have not made public elsewhere.

Every row carries a way to report it and a way to block the person, and the moderation process is
written on the page rather than described somewhere else. A rank on it is a rank among the people who
chose to be listed, and the page says so; it is not a rank among everyone using ClearHire.

## What it does not do

- **It does not change hiring outcomes.** Employers do not see your badges, your level or your rank.
- **It does not decay.** No streak, no expiry, nothing removed for inactivity.
- **It is not a requirement.** Every core feature works whether or not you look at this at all.

## FAQ

### Does ClearHire have streaks?

No, and it never has. The design rules them out by name: *"There is no streak to break and nothing
decays."* Points come from finishing something, not from turning up.

### Do employers see my badges or level?

No. They record what you did on ClearHire. They are not shown to employers and are not a
qualification.

### How do I get points?

By finishing a challenge. Opening the app earns nothing, and nothing you have earned is ever taken
back.

### Is the leaderboard on by default?

No. You turn it on, the page tells you exactly what it will publish before you do, and you can turn
it off again.

### Can I report someone on the leaderboard?

Yes. Every row that is not your own carries a report control and a block control, and the page
explains what happens after you use either.
